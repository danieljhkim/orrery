# Lattice two-source superposition

## Declared test

Question: on a finite shared counting lattice, does an extended background
depletion multiply the stored-scarcity gradient generated by an embedded
compact source?

The observable is the signed projection of the compact source's paired radial
scarcity gradient onto its isolated-source gradient, measured over radii 4–18
cells. Independence predicts amplitude 1; the proposed multiplicative
enhancement predicts approximately `1/(1-D)`; anonymous finite-budget counting
predicts screening because compact-source hits can remove only slots that
survive the background. Near exhaustion the full spatial profile, not only a
binary verdict, is recorded. A result supports enhancement only if the measured
amplitude grows with depletion beyond counting noise and resolution error.

## Microscopic apparatus

The 129×129 square lattice has 50,000 anonymous slots per cell and a full outer
reservoir. Five-point lattice Poisson solves generate exposure maps for a
uniform extended disk (radius 0.31 lattice widths) and a compact one-cell source
at its center. These maps specify where depletion attempts arrive; they do not
specify how two sources superpose.

Every background attempt chooses a slot without knowing its source or state.
For Poisson exposure `H`, a slot survives with probability `exp(-H)`, so the
endpoint count is sampled exactly as `Binomial(C, exp(-H))`. Compact attempts
then act on those same survivors. Paired subtraction therefore identifies the
compact source while preserving the shared finite budget and its hard
saturation. The seed is 42; each of 12 ambient levels uses 32 independent
finite-count replicates. Sizes 97 and 161 repeat the exact expectation with
geometrically scaled source and measurement radii.

Run:

```text
uv run lab/sims/lattice-two-source-superposition/main.py
```

The command rewrites `assets/results.json` and `assets/superposition.png`.
`results.json` is the authoritative full-precision report.

## Result

The compact field is screened, not enhanced. Over ambient depletion
`D=0.01–0.90`, the exact lattice measurement is

```text
A(D) = (1-D)^1.07079
```

with constrained `A(0)=1`. Its log-RMSE is 0.00308, versus 0.0711 for the
simple local-headroom prediction, 1.074 for independence, and 2.077 for
`1/(1-D)`. The exponent is slightly steeper than one because the extended
background is not spatially constant: as its amplitude increases, headroom
changes across the 4–18-cell gradient measurement annulus.

At 80%, 90%, 96%, 98.5%, and 99.5% central depletion, the source-field
amplitude is respectively 0.1790, 0.08451, 0.03096, 0.01037, and 0.002948 of
its isolated value. Thus the exhaustion crossover is continuous suppression,
well described by a headroom power through 90% and increasingly steepened by
the well's spatial variation thereafter; no upturn appears. The total compact
sink delivered across the lattice falls more slowly than the local field
(to 0.08991 at 99.5%) because outer cells retain more headroom.

All 12 seeded finite-count means lie within 2.31 standard errors of the exact
binomial expectation. Repeating the expectation on 97×97 and 161×161 lattices
gives exponents 1.07250 and 1.06999; the largest field-amplitude change from
129×129 is 0.000444 and 0.000206 respectively.

This is an apparatus-level result for anonymous, fixed-coupling depletion
attempts. A rule that explicitly changes a source's attempt rate with ambient
occupancy is a different microscopic model and is not inferred merely from a
shared budget. Reconciliation with the principia theory ledger belongs to
kepler.
